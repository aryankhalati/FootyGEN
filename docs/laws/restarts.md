# Throw-ins, Goal Kicks and Corners

## Summary
Throw-ins, goal kicks and corner kicks are the restarts used when the whole ball crosses a boundary line. A throw-in is used when it crosses the touchline. A goal kick or corner kick is used when it crosses the goal line without a goal being scored. They are covered by Laws 15, 16 and 17 of the Laws of the Game. The ball counts as out only when all of it has crossed the line, whether on the ground or in the air.

## How is a throw-in taken?
A throw-in is awarded to the opponents of the player who last touched the ball. The thrower faces the field and must have part of each foot on the touchline or on the ground outside it. The ball is thrown with both hands from behind and over the head, from the place where it left the field. The ball is in play as soon as it enters the field. Opponents must stay at least 2 metres from the point of the throw-in.

## What are the throw-in restrictions?
If a throw-in is taken improperly, the throw goes to the opponents. The thrower cannot touch the ball again until another player has touched it. A goal cannot be scored directly from a throw-in. If the ball goes into the opponents' goal, a goal kick is given. If it goes into the thrower's own goal, a corner kick is given to the opponents.If the referee thinks a throw-in is taking too long or is being deliberately delayed, a five-second visual countdown starts, and if the ball is not in play when it ends, the throw-in goes to the opponents.

## How is a goal kick taken?
A goal kick is awarded when the whole ball crosses the goal line after last being touched by an attacker, and no goal is scored. The ball must be stationary and is kicked from anywhere inside the goal area by a player of the defending team. Opponents must stay outside the penalty area until the ball is in play. The ball is in play as soon as it is kicked and clearly moves. It does not have to leave the penalty area. The kicker may not touch it again until another player has.If the referee thinks a goal kick is being deliberately delayed, a five-second visual countdown starts, and if the ball is not in play when it ends, the opponents get a corner kick.

## How is a corner kick taken?
A corner kick is awarded when the whole ball crosses the goal line after last being touched by a defender, including the goalkeeper, and no goal is scored. The ball is placed in the corner area nearest to where it went out. The corner flag must not be moved. The ball is in play when it is kicked and clearly moves, and it does not have to leave the corner area. Opponents must stay at least 9.15 metres (10 yards) away until it is in play. The kicker may not touch it again until another player has.

## Can a goal be scored directly from these restarts?
A goal can be scored directly from a goal kick or a corner kick, but only against the opponents. If the ball goes directly into the kicker's own goal, the opponents are awarded a corner kick. As noted in the offside doc, a player cannot be penalised for offside by receiving the ball directly from a throw-in, a goal kick or a corner kick.
