# Match Duration, Extra Time, Shootouts and Substitutions

## Summary
A standard match has two equal halves and ends when the referee blows the final whistle. If a competition needs a winner after a draw, its rules may add extra time, a penalty shoot-out, or both. Competition rules also set how many substitutes can be named and used. The Laws of the Game cover match duration in Law 7, substitutions in Law 3 and the ways to decide a drawn match in Law 10.

## How long is a match?
A match is played in two equal halves of 45 minutes, unless the referee and both teams agree otherwise before it starts and the competition rules allow it. Players are entitled to an interval at half-time that does not exceed 15 minutes. Competition rules can set the exact length of the interval. The clock is not stopped when the ball goes out of play, so the referee makes up for lost time at the end of each half.

## What is stoppage time?
Stoppage time, also called added time, is extra time added to the end of each half to make up for time lost during play. The referee decides how much to add. Common causes are substitutions, injuries, time wasting, disciplinary actions, goal celebrations, and delays for VAR checks and reviews. The fourth official often shows the minimum added time on a board. If a penalty kick is awarded, time is added so it can be taken and completed.

## What is extra time?
Extra time is a period played after a drawn match when a competition needs a winner. The Laws allow it but do not require it. Competition rules decide whether it is used, and it is commonly two periods of 15 minutes. If the score is still level after extra time, the competition rules may then use a penalty shoot-out.

## How does a penalty shoot-out work?
A shoot-out is a set of penalty kicks used to decide the winner of a drawn match. The coin toss decides which goal is used and which team kicks first. The teams take kicks alternately, five each to start. The shoot-out stops early if one team can no longer catch up. If the score is level after five kicks each, play continues in equal numbers of kicks until one team leads. Only players on the field at the end of the match, including extra time, or temporarily off it, can take part. Goals scored in a shoot-out are not added to the match score.

## How do substitutions work?
Competition rules decide how many substitutes can be named. The Laws allow up to five substitutions in a match, made in a limited number of stoppages, with half-time not counted. The referee must be told before a substitution is made. The player coming off leaves first, and the substitute enters only at the halfway line after the referee gives permission. A player who has been substituted off cannot take further part in the match.A substituted player must leave the field within 10 seconds of the substitution board being shown, or of the referee's signal. If the player is slow, the substitute cannot enter until the first stoppage after one minute of play. A player who is assessed or treated on the field, or whose injury stops play, must also leave and stay off for one minute of play after the restart, with some exceptions.
