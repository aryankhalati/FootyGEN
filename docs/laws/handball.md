# Handball Rules

## Summary
Handball is part of Law 12 (Fouls and Misconduct). Not every contact between the ball and a player's hand or arm is an offence. The referee judges whether the contact was deliberate, and whether the player's arm position made their body unnaturally bigger. The usual punishment is a direct free kick, or a penalty kick if the offence happens inside the defender's own penalty area.

## What counts as the hand and arm?
For handball, the upper edge of the arm is the bottom of the armpit. Contact with the shoulder is not handball. The arm below that line, down to the fingertips, counts. The same boundary applies to every outfield player and to goalkeepers outside their own penalty area.

## When is handball an offence?
A player commits an offence by deliberately touching the ball with the hand or arm, including by moving the hand or arm towards the ball. It is also an offence to hit the ball with something held, such as a boot or shinguard thrown at it. A player can also be penalised if the ball touches a hand or arm that has made the body unnaturally bigger. A hand or arm above shoulder level is not normally seen as a natural position for the movement the player is making.

## Accidental contact
The referee looks at several things before penalising contact. One is whether the hand moved towards the ball, or the ball moved towards the hand. Another is how far away the ball was played from, because a ball arriving unexpectedly from very close gives the player little time to react. A hand in a particular position is not automatically an offence. A player who falls and uses a hand or arm to support their body between the body and the ground is not normally penalised, as long as the arm is not extended sideways or vertically.

## Goalkeepers
Inside their own penalty area, goalkeepers may use their hands, with limits. They cannot handle the ball after a teammate has deliberately kicked it to them, or after a throw-in from a teammate. The punishment is an indirect free kick. A goalkeeper also cannot control the ball with their hands or arms for more than eight seconds. The referee shows a visual five-second countdown, and if the goalkeeper still has the ball when it ends, the opponents are awarded a corner kick instead of an indirect free kick. Outside their own penalty area, goalkeepers are treated like any other player for handball.

## Goals and handball
A goal scored directly from a player's hand or arm is disallowed, even if the contact was accidental. This is separate from the question of whether the contact was deliberate.

## Punishment and discipline
A handball offence is punished with a direct free kick, or a penalty kick if it happens inside the offender's own penalty area. A player who deliberately handles the ball to stop a promising attack is cautioned with a yellow card. A player who deliberately handles the ball to deny a goal or an obvious goal-scoring opportunity is sent off with a red card.
