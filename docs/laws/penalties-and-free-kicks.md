# Penalty Kicks and Free Kick Rules

## Summary
Penalty kicks are covered by Law 14 and free kicks by Law 13 of the Laws of the Game. A penalty kick is awarded for a direct free kick offence by the defending team inside its own penalty area. Other offences lead to free kicks. Which offences lead to a direct or an indirect free kick is covered in the fouls and cards doc. This doc covers how the kicks are taken.

## Where is a penalty kick taken from?
A penalty kick is taken from the penalty mark, which is 11 metres (12 yards) from the goal line and midway between the posts. The ball must be stationary and the kicker clearly identified. The ball must be kicked forward, and the kicker may not touch it again until another player has. A goal can be scored directly from a penalty kick.

## What must players do at a penalty kick?
The defending goalkeeper must stay on the goal line, between the posts and facing the kicker, until the ball is kicked. At least part of one foot must be touching or in line with the goal line. All other players must be on the field, outside the penalty area and behind the penalty mark, at least 9.15 metres (10 yards) from it. The kicker may feint during the run-up, but feinting once the run-up is complete is an offence.

## What happens if a rule is broken at a penalty?
The result depends on who broke the rule and whether a goal was scored. If the goalkeeper breaks the rules and the kick is saved or misses, it is retaken. If a goal is scored, it stands. If a teammate of the kicker encroaches and the ball goes in, the kick is retaken. If it does not go in, the defending team gets an indirect free kick. If the kicker breaks the rules after the ball is kicked, the defending team also gets an indirect free kick.

## How is a free kick taken?
The ball must be stationary, and the kicker may not touch it again until another player has. The ball is in play once it is kicked and clearly moves. A free kick is taken from where the offence happened. There is one exception: an indirect free kick for the attackers inside the opponents' goal area is taken from the nearest point on the goal area line parallel to the goal line. A free kick for the defending team inside its own goal area can be taken from anywhere in that area.

## How far away must opponents stand?
Opponents must be at least 9.15 metres (10 yards) from the ball until it is in play. The exception is a player standing on their own goal line between the posts. If the free kick is taken inside the defending team's own penalty area, all opponents must stay outside the penalty area until the ball is in play. The ball does not need to leave the area. If a defensive wall of three or more players is formed, attackers must stay at least 1 metre away from it.

## Can a free kick be taken quickly?
Yes. A free kick can be taken quickly, even if opponents are closer than 9.15 metres. If one of them intercepts the ball, the referee allows play to continue.
