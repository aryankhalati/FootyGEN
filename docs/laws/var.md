# What VAR Can and Cannot Review

## Summary
VAR stands for video assistant referee. It is a match official who watches video and helps the referee in specific situations. The rules for it are set out in the VAR protocol, which is part of the Laws of the Game. VAR is meant to correct clear mistakes in a small number of important decisions, not to review every call. Competitions decide whether to use it, and the referee always makes the final decision.

## What can VAR review?

There are four types of decision. The first is goals, including offences by the attacking team in the build-up, such as offside, a foul or handball. The second is penalty decisions, whether a penalty is given or not given. The third is direct red card incidents. Since the 2026/27 Laws took effect on 1 July 2026, VAR can also review a red card that came from a clearly incorrect second yellow card. The fourth is mistaken identity, when the referee cautions or sends off the wrong player, including when the offence was committed by a player of either team. Competitions can also choose to let VAR review a clearly incorrectly awarded corner kick, if the decision can be corrected immediately and without delaying the restart.

## What does clear and obvious error mean?
For judgement calls, such as whether a challenge was a foul or how serious it was, VAR should step in only if the referee made a clear and obvious error. A close or debatable call is left alone. Factual matters are different. Whether a player was in an offside position, or whether contact happened inside or outside the penalty area, can be checked against the video.

## How does a review work?
The VAR and assistants watch from a video room and check every incident in the four categories in the background. If there is no error, the referee is not interrupted and play continues. If there is a possible error, the VAR tells the referee by headset. The referee can accept the information, or go to a monitor at the side of the pitch to watch the footage. A referee signals a review by drawing a rectangle in the air.

## What can VAR not review?

VAR cannot review decisions outside these categories. These include yellow cards, throw-ins, goal kicks and most free kicks, unless the decision is part of a build-up to a goal. A second yellow card is not reviewed in general. VAR can step in only when it led to a red card and was clearly incorrect. A corner kick can be reviewed only in competitions that choose to allow it. Under the standard protocol, players and coaches cannot ask for a review. Once play has restarted, a review is normally no longer possible, with limited exceptions such as mistaken identity.

## Who makes the final decision?
The referee does. VAR can only advise, and the referee either keeps the original decision or changes it. VAR cannot overrule the referee directly. This is why a referee may change a decision after looking at the monitor, or may stay with the original call even when the VAR suggests a review.
