# Full-Back Role and Variations

## Summary
A full-back is a wide defender on the left or right of the back line. Most teams play two full-backs in a back four. The role combines defending the flank with supporting attacks down the touchline. Full-backs can overlap or underlap, so the role changes from team to team. A full-back needs both defensive discipline and the stamina to cover a lot of ground.

## What does a full-back do in defence?
A full-back defends the wide area, marks the opposing winger and stops crosses. They tackle, block shots and cover the space beside the centre-backs. When the ball is on the far side, the full-back moves in to protect the middle. Good positioning matters as much as tackling. A full-back who gets too far forward leaves space behind, so they must judge when to attack and when to stay back.

## What does a full-back do in build-up?
In build-up, a full-back is often the wide option for the centre-backs and the goalkeeper. They can receive a pass near the touchline, play it forward to a winger or midfielder, or pass back inside. Where they stand affects the team's shape. A full-back who stays deep gives a safe outlet, and one who moves higher helps the team move the ball up the pitch. They must also check for pressure, because a lost ball near their own box is dangerous.

## What does a full-back do in attack?
A full-back offers width, receives passes from midfield and the winger, and delivers crosses or cut-backs. They can also pass inside to a midfielder or play a one-two with a winger. In a 4-3-3, the winger often stays wide to stretch the defence, and the full-back supports from behind. Full-backs add an extra player to the attack, which can create a two-versus-one against the opposing full-back.

## What are overlapping and underlapping?
An overlapping full-back runs outside the winger on the touchline and gives an option down the line. This works well when the winger cuts inside. An underlapping full-back runs inside the winger, into the half-space between the wide area and the centre. This can pull a midfielder out of position and open a passing lane. Which run is better depends on where the winger plays and how the opposing full-back defends.

## Why does rest defence matter for full-backs?
Rest defence means the players kept back to stop a counter-attack. If both full-backs go forward at the same time, the centre-backs and the No. 6 can be left exposed in the wide channels. Teams often send only one full-back forward and keep the other back. Which full-back advances can depend on where the ball is and which midfielder has moved forward.

## What qualities does a full-back need?
The role needs stamina, because the player defends and attacks along the same flank for the whole match. In defence, one-on-one tackling, good positioning and the pace to recover when beaten matter most. In attack, accurate crossing and passing let the full-back use the width they give the team. A full-back also needs to understand the winger in front of them, because the two must decide together who stays wide and who moves inside. Scanning before receiving the ball helps the full-back choose between a safe pass and a forward one.
