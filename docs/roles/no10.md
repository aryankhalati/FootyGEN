# Playmaker Between the Lines (No. 10) Role

## Summary
The No. 10 is a creative attacking midfielder who plays behind the striker and in front of the midfield. The main job is to create chances for teammates, usually by receiving the ball between the opposition's midfield and defence. In a 4-2-3-1 it is a clearly defined position behind the striker.

## What does between the lines mean?
The lines are the opposition's defence and midfield. The space between them is where a No. 10 looks to receive the ball. A player there can turn and face the defence, which forces defenders to step out or stay deep. If a centre-back steps out, space opens behind. If the defenders stay, the No. 10 has time to pass or shoot. The movement is the core of the role.

## What does a No. 10 do in possession?
A No. 10 finds pockets of space, receives the ball, and plays the final pass. The player links the midfield to the striker and the wingers, plays through balls, and combines with teammates near the box. Shots from the edge of the area are also part of the job. The No. 10 must scan for space before receiving so the first touch can face the goal.

## How does a No. 10 fit different systems?
A 4-2-3-1 has a No. 10 in the middle of the three behind the striker, with two deeper midfielders behind. A 4-3-3 does not have a fixed No. 10, but an advanced No. 8 can play a similar role. A false 9 who drops into midfield uses the same space. Teams choose based on the players they have.

## What does a No. 10 do without the ball?
The amount of defending depends on the team. Some teams ask the No. 10 to press the opposition's holding midfielder or block passes into the centre. Others ask the player to stay higher so the team can counter-attack quickly after winning the ball. A No. 10 who does not defend leaves the rest of the team to cover more space, so the team's shape must be balanced.

## Who is an example of a No. 10?
Mesut Özil is often named as an example of a No. 10, known for creating chances for teammates. He played this kind of role for Real Madrid and Arsenal.

## What are the risks of the No. 10 role?
A No. 10 can be marked tightly by the opposition's holding midfielder, which removes the space they need. The role also depends on teammates. If the striker and wingers do not move, the No. 10 may have no good passing options. The team also needs the other midfielders to cover defensive space, because a No. 10 usually does less defending than a No. 6 or No. 8.

## What qualities does a No. 10 need?
The role needs vision and creativity, because the player must see passing options before the defence closes them. A good first touch, close control in tight spaces and accurate through balls matter most. A No. 10 also needs to find space between the lines and scan before receiving the ball, so that the first touch can turn and face the goal. Shooting from the edge of the area adds a second way to threaten the defence.
