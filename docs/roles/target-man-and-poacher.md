# Target Man and Poacher Roles

## Summary
The target man and the poacher are two types of central striker. A target man is a strong forward who holds up the ball and gives the team a place to play it. A poacher is a forward who stays close to goal and scores from chances in the box. They do different jobs for the team. A target man helps the team move up the pitch, and a poacher finishes the chances that the team creates.

## What is a target man?
A target man is a forward who is strong in the air and on the ground. The player stands with their back to goal, receives long passes or crosses, and holds off defenders. From there the target man can lay the ball off to a midfielder, flick it on to a runner or turn and shoot. Teammates can then run past or around the striker. The role also helps at set pieces, where the target man can win headers.

## What does a poacher do?
A poacher stays close to the goal and waits for chances. The player does not take part in build-up as much as other forwards. Instead, a poacher makes short, well-timed runs into the penalty area, to meet crosses and cut-backs and to be ready for rebounds and loose balls. Positioning and timing matter most. Many chances come from first-time finishes inside the box.

## How do the two roles compare?
A target man helps the team progress and keep the ball in the final third. A poacher is judged mainly on goals and shots from close range. A target man works with their back to goal and a poacher faces goal. A target man also takes part more in play outside the box, and a poacher usually stays inside it. Some strikers have both skills, but the roles are different.

## How do they fit into a team?
A target man suits a team that plays long passes or crosses, because the striker can win the first ball. A poacher suits a team that creates chances through wingers, full-backs or midfield runners, because the striker gets service in the box. A traditional No. 9 can do either job or both. Each type needs teammates who provide the right passes, so the choice depends on how the team attacks.

## Who is an example of a poacher?
Cristiano Ronaldo in the later part of his career is often cited as an example of a striker who was mainly a finisher in the penalty area. He earlier played wide as a forward, but later played closer to goal.

## What are the risks of each role?
A target man can struggle if the team does not deliver good long balls or crosses, because the striker is then isolated. A poacher can have little influence on the game if the team does not create chances, and may be less involved in build-up and pressing.

## What qualities do a target man and a poacher need?
A target man needs physical strength and aerial ability, because the role depends on winning headers and holding off defenders. A secure first touch with their back to goal and accurate lay-offs to teammates matter most. A poacher needs sharp positioning and timing, because the chances come from being in the right place a moment before the defenders. Composure and quick first-time finishing in the box, plus anticipation of rebounds and loose balls, matter most for the poacher. Both types also need good movement to find space and the ability to finish when the chance arrives.
