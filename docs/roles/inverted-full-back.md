# Inverted Full-Back Role

## Summary
An inverted full-back is a full-back who moves into central midfield when the team has the ball, instead of staying wide. In defence the player returns to the back line. The role is a way to add another central player in possession while the winger keeps the width. It is linked with possession football and positional play. It changes the shape of the team, so teammates must adjust their positions.

## What does an inverted full-back do?
When the team builds from the back, the full-back steps inside next to the No. 6 or ahead of the centre-backs. From there the player can receive passes between the lines and pass forward. This can turn a back four into a back three with two midfielders in front, usually called a 3-2-5 when the team attacks.

## Why do teams use it?
The main reason is control of the centre. An extra central player gives the team more passing options in midfield. It also helps when the ball is lost. Players already positioned in the middle can win it back quickly, and the team is more compact against a counter-attack. Manchester City under Pep Guardiola is a well-known example of a team that used this idea.

## What does it change for teammates?
If the full-back moves inside, the winger must stay wide to keep the team's width. The No. 8s can then make forward runs, because the full-back helps cover the middle. The other full-back may stay back, overlap, or also move inside. Each flank can use a different solution. If both the winger and the full-back move inside, nobody is left wide.

## What are the risks?
The main risk is the space left on the flank. If the team loses the ball while the full-back is in midfield, the opponent can attack the wide channel, and the full-back is not in a natural position to defend it. This is why rest defence matters, with the other defenders and midfielders keeping a good shape behind the ball. A full-back who is not comfortable under pressure in midfield can lose the ball in a dangerous area.

## What qualities does an inverted full-back need?
The player needs to be comfortable on the ball and able to receive it with an opponent nearby. Good passing, awareness of space and decision-making matter in this role. The player must also read the game well, to know when to move inside and when to stay wide. Tackling and positioning are still needed, because the player must defend the flank when the team loses the ball.
