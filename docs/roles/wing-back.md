# Wing-Back Role

## Summary
A wing-back is a wide player in a team that plays three centre-backs. The wing-back covers the whole flank, from defending near their own box to attacking near the opponents' goal line. The role is a mix of full-back and winger. It is used in formations such as 3-5-2, 3-4-3 and 5-3-2. Because the wing-back covers so much ground, the role needs a lot of stamina.

## What does a wing-back do in attack?
In attack, a wing-back gives the team its width, usually playing high up the touchline. They receive passes from midfield, run past defenders, and deliver crosses or cut-backs. A wing-back can also move inside to combine with the forwards or midfielders.

## What does a wing-back do in defence?
When the team loses the ball, the wing-back drops back to defend the flank. In many teams the back three becomes a back five, with the wing-backs joining the centre-backs. The wing-back then marks the opposing winger or wide player and stops crosses. A wing-back who is caught high up the pitch leaves space behind, so the wide centre-back often has to cover.

## Which formations use wing-backs?
Wing-backs appear in back-three systems. In a 3-5-2 and a 3-4-3, the wing-backs cover the wide areas of the midfield line, so the team relies on them for width. A 5-3-2 is a more defensive version, with the wing-backs staying closer to the back line. The same players can switch between these shapes during a match, depending on whether the team has the ball.

## How is a wing-back different from a full-back?
A full-back plays in a back four, alongside two centre-backs. A wing-back plays in a back three or back five and is usually the only wide player on that side of the midfield. The wing-back must therefore provide width and defend the wide area. A wing-back is expected to attack and defend along the whole touchline.

## What are the risks of the wing-back role?
If the wing-back goes forward and the team loses the ball, the flank can be exposed to a counter-attack. A wing-back who is isolated against two opponents can also be outnumbered. The team needs the wide centre-backs and a central midfielder to cover when the wing-back is high.

## What qualities does a wing-back need?
The role needs exceptional stamina, because the wing-back runs up and down the whole flank for the entire match. Accurate crossing, pace and the ability to beat a defender one-on-one matter most in attack. In defence, tackling and positioning matter, along with the discipline to return quickly when the team loses the ball, since the flank behind is open. A wing-back also needs good communication with the wide centre-back, so the two know who covers when the wing-back is high. Scanning before receiving the ball helps the wing-back choose between a cross, a pass inside or a run forward.
