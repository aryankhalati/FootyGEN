# Holding Midfielder (No. 6) Role

## Summary
The holding midfielder, often called the No. 6, plays in front of the back line and behind the other midfielders. The job is to protect the centre of the pitch and link defence to midfield. In a 4-3-3 the No. 6 is usually the deepest of the three midfielders. The role combines defending space, winning the ball and giving the team a safe passing option.

## What does a No. 6 do in defence?
A No. 6 shields the back four by standing between the opposition's midfield and the centre-backs. The player blocks passing lanes into the striker or the advanced midfielders, wins tackles and interceptions, and covers space when a centre-back or full-back steps out. If the No. 6 stays in the right place, opponents have little room to receive the ball between the lines.

## What does a No. 6 do in possession?
In possession, the No. 6 gives the centre-backs and goalkeeper a passing outlet in front of them. The player can receive the ball, turn, and pass forward or switch it to the other side. The No. 6 may also move to one side to open a passing lane, or drop between the centre-backs. If the No. 6 drops, a No. 8 often moves deeper or stays high to keep a presence in the middle.

## How is a No. 6 different from a deep-lying playmaker?
A deep-lying playmaker, or DLP, plays in a similar deep position but is judged mainly on passing. The DLP sets the tempo, switches play and finds forward passes from deep. A No. 6 is judged mainly on protecting the space in front of the defence. Some players do both jobs, and one player can be a No. 6 in one team and a deep-lying playmaker in another.

## How does the No. 6 fit different systems?
A 4-3-3 with one No. 6 and two No. 8s is the common version. Some teams use two deeper midfielders and one advanced one, which can look like a 4-2-3-1 in play. When the team defends, the No. 6 sits in front of the back four, which gives a 4-1-4-1 if the other midfielders and wingers drop back. Busquets in Guardiola's Barcelona is a well-known example of a No. 6.

## What is the No. 6's role in rest defence?
When the team attacks, the No. 6 is usually one of the players kept behind the ball to stop a counter-attack, together with the centre-backs. If both full-backs have gone forward, the No. 6 can be left alone to cover a lot of space. The No. 6 must also read when to stay central and when to cover the wide channels.

## What are the risks of a lone No. 6?
A single No. 6 can be overloaded if the No. 8s push too high or if opponents put several players around them. Gaps can also open between the lines if the forwards press and the midfield cannot follow. A No. 6 who loses the ball in a deep position can give the opponent a direct chance. The team needs the No. 8s and the centre-backs to support the position.

## What qualities does a No. 6 need?
The role needs good positional sense, because the player must always know where the space and the opponents are. Passing under pressure, tackling and the ability to read the game matter most. A No. 6 also scans for opponents before receiving the ball, so that the first touch can play the team forward.
