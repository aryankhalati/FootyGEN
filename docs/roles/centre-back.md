# Centre-Back Duties, Ball-Playing vs Stopper

## Summary
A centre-back plays in the middle of the defence, in front of the goalkeeper. Most teams play two centre-backs in a back four, and some play three. The main jobs are marking the opposition's centre-forwards, winning headers, blocking shots and clearing danger. Modern centre-backs are also expected to pass and help the team build attacks. Two common styles are the stopper, who defends aggressively, and the ball-playing centre-back, who is strong on the ball.

## What does a centre-back do?
A centre-back marks forwards, wins aerial duels, tackles, blocks shots and crosses, and clears the ball when under pressure. They also cover space behind teammates and organise the defensive line. Communication is part of the job, because the centre-backs can see the play in front of them. On set pieces they defend the box, and at corners and free kicks they may also attack the ball in the opponents' box.

## How does a centre-back manage the defensive line?
The centre-backs set the height of the back line, together with the goalkeeper and full-backs. A high line squeezes the pitch and can catch attackers offside. A deeper line gives less space behind the defence. The line must move together. If one defender steps up and another stays back, a forward can be played onside. When one centre-back steps out to challenge, the other covers the space behind him.

## What is a stopper?
A stopper is a centre-back who steps out of the line to challenge the forward before they can turn or receive the ball. The aim is to win the ball early or stop the attack at its source. Strong tackling, aggression and good timing matter in this role. The risk is the space left behind. If a stopper is beaten or arrives late, the forward may run through on goal unless a partner covers.

## What is a ball-playing centre-back?
A ball-playing centre-back is comfortable receiving under pressure and moving the ball forward. They can pass through the opposition's lines, play long diagonal passes to the wings, or carry the ball into midfield. In a 4-3-3 that builds from the back, they work with the goalkeeper and the No. 6. If the No. 6 drops between the centre-backs, they may split wider to make room. The risk is a mistake in a dangerous area, which can lead directly to a chance for the opponent.

## How do the two styles work together?
Many teams pair different types of centre-back, with one more aggressive and one who covers and passes. The stopper steps out to challenge, and the partner covers behind him and starts attacks. A pair of similar players can work if the team's style matches. A team that presses high needs centre-backs who can defend open space. A team that builds patiently needs centre-backs who can pass. Most modern centre-backs are expected to do both jobs to some degree.

## What qualities does a centre-back need?
The role needs strong positioning and concentration, because one lapse can lead directly to a chance. Aerial ability, tackling and pace to recover against fast forwards matter most. A centre-back also needs good communication to organise the line, and composure on the ball to pass under pressure. Scanning before receiving the ball helps the player choose a safe pass or a forward one.
