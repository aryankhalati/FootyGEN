# Central Midfielder (No. 8) and Box-to-Box Role

## Summary
The No. 8 is a central midfielder who plays ahead of the holding midfielder and behind the forwards. In a 4-3-3 there are usually two No. 8s. They connect the lines, carry the ball forward and make runs into the box. A box-to-box midfielder is a type of No. 8 who contributes in both penalty areas, defending in one and attacking in the other.

## What does a No. 8 do in possession?
A No. 8 receives the ball from defenders and the No. 6, and moves it forward by passing or carrying. In a 4-3-3, the No. 8s form passing triangles with the No. 6, the full-backs and the wingers. They also join attacks with late runs into the box, which can catch defenders unprepared. If the No. 6 drops between the centre-backs, a No. 8 often moves deeper or stays high to keep a presence in the middle.

## What does a No. 8 do without the ball?
Without the ball, the No. 8s stay close behind the front three. They cut central passing lanes and move up when the team presses. Pressing works when the whole team moves together, so a No. 8 must time the move with the forwards. If the press fails, the No. 8 drops back to protect the centre and the space in front of the back four.

## What is a box-to-box midfielder?
A box-to-box midfielder covers the pitch from one penalty area to the other. The player helps defend near their own box, wins the ball, and then carries it or runs forward to support the attack. The role needs stamina and good decisions about when to go forward and when to stay back. Not every No. 8 plays box-to-box. Some focus more on passing and linking play than on running.

## How do two No. 8s share the work?
The two No. 8s do not need identical jobs. In one version, one pushes into the box while the other stays deeper. In another, one creates while the other presses and covers. In many 4-3-3 teams the No. 8s take turns advancing so the centre is never left empty. Xavi and Iniesta played in midfield for Guardiola's Barcelona, and Kroos and Modrić played in midfield for Real Madrid during the three-peat era.

## How is a No. 8 different from a No. 6?
A No. 6 stays deeper and protects the space in front of the back four. A No. 8 plays further forward and has more freedom to join the attack. The two roles must balance each other. If both No. 8s go forward at once, the No. 6 is left alone in the centre. The No. 8s also need to keep their distance from the No. 6, so the team is not stretched or split.

## What are the risks of the No. 8 role?
A No. 8 who advances too far leaves the No. 6 exposed, especially if opponents put several players around them. The role also demands a lot of running, so a tired No. 8 can leave gaps between the lines. Gaps can also open if the forwards press and the midfield cannot follow. Teams manage this by agreeing which No. 8 goes forward and which stays back.

## What qualities does a No. 8 need?
The role needs stamina, because the player covers a lot of ground in both directions over 90 minutes. Passing in tight spaces, carrying the ball past pressure and good timing of runs into the box matter most. A No. 8 also needs positional sense, so that the player knows when to advance and when to stay back to protect the No. 6. Scanning before receiving the ball helps the No. 8 turn and play the team forward.
