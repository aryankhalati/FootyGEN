# Carlo Ancelotti: Man-Management and Tactical Flexibility

## Summary
Carlo Ancelotti is an Italian football manager. He played in midfield for Roma and AC Milan, and later managed Milan, Chelsea, Paris Saint-Germain, Real Madrid, Bayern Munich, Napoli and Everton. He is known for calm leadership, for keeping star players working together, and for changing his shape to suit his squad. He has won the Champions League as a manager five times.

## What is his philosophy?
Ancelotti does not tie his teams to one system. He looks at the players he has and builds the shape around their strengths. He gives senior players trust and responsibility, and he keeps the dressing room calm. His teams are balanced between attack and defence, and they often rely on strong individual players working inside a clear structure.

## How did his Milan team play?
Ancelotti managed Milan from 2001 to 2009. The team often played a 4-3-2-1, nicknamed the "Christmas tree", with Andrea Pirlo as a deep-lying playmaker in front of the defence, in a role close to a No. 6. Kaká, Clarence Seedorf and Gennaro Gattuso played in midfield, and Paolo Maldini and Alessandro Nesta defended. Milan won the Champions League in 2002-03, beating Juventus on penalties, and again in 2006-07, beating Liverpool in Athens.

## How did he win La Decima with Real Madrid?
Ancelotti's first spell at Real Madrid ran from 2013 to 2015. In 2013-14, Madrid won the Champions League for the tenth time, known as La Decima. They beat Atlético Madrid in the final in Lisbon, after Sergio Ramos scored an equaliser in stoppage time. Cristiano Ronaldo, Gareth Bale and Karim Benzema led the attack, and Luka Modrić and Xabi Alonso supported them in midfield.

## What did he win in his second spell at Madrid?
Ancelotti returned to Real Madrid in 2021 and left in 2025. His teams won La Liga and the Champions League in 2021-22, beating Liverpool in the final in Paris, and again in 2023-24, beating Borussia Dortmund at Wembley. In the first of those seasons, Madrid came back from behind in several knockout matches.

## How did he adapt his tactics?
In his second spell, Madrid usually played a 4-3-3 with Casemiro, Toni Kroos and Modrić in midfield. After Casemiro left in 2022 and Benzema in 2023, he adjusted again. In 2023-24 he used Jude Bellingham in a more advanced role, with no traditional centre-forward in some matches. This kind of change between seasons is a clear example of his flexibility.

## What are the main criticisms?
Some critics say his teams depend too much on individual quality, and that his relaxed style gives less structure than other top managers. Supporters point to his trophies and to how well he gets the best from large squads.
