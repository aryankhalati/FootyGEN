# Pep Guardiola: Philosophy and Teams

## Summary
Pep Guardiola is a Spanish football manager. He played in midfield for Barcelona and later managed Barcelona, Bayern Munich and Manchester City. His teams are known for controlling matches through possession, careful positioning and quick pressure after losing the ball. He is closely linked with positional play and with tiki-taka, although he has often been described as a coach who adapts his ideas to his players.

## What is his philosophy?
Guardiola builds his teams around keeping the ball and controlling where the game is played. His teams build from the back, using the goalkeeper as a passing option. Players take up set positions in the lanes of the pitch, so there are always passing options and the opponent has to move around. His teams also press straight after losing the ball, to win it back high up the pitch. His playing career under Johan Cruyff at Barcelona shaped his ideas.

## How did his Barcelona team play?
Guardiola managed Barcelona from 2008 to 2012. The team played in a 4-3-3, with Sergio Busquets as the No. 6 and Xavi and Andrés Iniesta in midfield. Lionel Messi often played as a false 9, dropping into midfield and creating an extra player in the centre. The team won the Champions League in 2009 and 2011, and it won six trophies in 2009.

## How did his Manchester City team play?
Guardiola managed Manchester City from 2016 until he left at the end of the 2025-26 season, after ten years in charge. The team kept possession, built from the back and used overloads and half-spaces to create chances. Guardiola changed his shape often, and players such as John Stones moved from defence into midfield during build-up. City won the Premier League, the FA Cup and the Champions League in 2022-23, a treble, and won four league titles in a row from 2020-21 to 2023-24. Enzo Maresca, who had been an assistant coach at City, replaced him in June 2026.

## How does he use his players?
Guardiola often asks players to take different roles in the same match. A defender can step into midfield, and a midfielder can move into the half-space. He has used a false 9 at Barcelona and a more traditional centre-forward at City, so his style is not tied to one type of player. The common idea is that every player understands the team's positions and passing options.

## What are the main criticisms?
Some critics say his teams can be too complicated, or that possession alone does not create chances against a deep low block. His teams also leave space behind when they press high, so a fast counter-attack can hurt them. Supporters point to the number of trophies his teams have won and the way other coaches have copied his methods.
