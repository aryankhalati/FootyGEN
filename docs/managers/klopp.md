# Jürgen Klopp: Gegenpressing Philosophy and Teams

## Summary
Jürgen Klopp is a German football manager. He managed Mainz, Borussia Dortmund and Liverpool. His teams are known for gegenpressing, which means pressing immediately after losing the ball, and for fast, direct attacks after winning it back. He is the manager most closely linked with this style.

## What is his philosophy?
Klopp builds his teams around winning the ball back high up the pitch and attacking before the opponent is organised. The moment after a turnover is treated as the best chance to create a shot, because the opponent's shape is broken. His teams press as a group, with short distances between the lines. They also play with a lot of energy and intensity, so fitness and teamwork are central to the idea.

## How did his Dortmund team play?
Klopp managed Borussia Dortmund from 2008 to 2015. The team won the Bundesliga in 2010-11 and 2011-12, and reached the Champions League final in 2013, which it lost to Bayern Munich. Dortmund pressed high, won the ball in advanced areas and attacked quickly with young, mobile players. Robert Lewandowski, Marco Reus and Mats Hummels were among the key players.

## How did his Liverpool team play?
Klopp became Liverpool manager in October 2015 and left at the end of the 2023-24 season. The team usually played a 4-3-3, with Mohamed Salah, Sadio Mané and Roberto Firmino in attack. The full-backs, Trent Alexander-Arnold and Andy Robertson, supplied much of the width and creativity. Virgil van Dijk led the defence, and Alisson was the goalkeeper. Liverpool won the Champions League in 2019 and the Premier League in 2019-20.

## How do his teams attack?
After winning the ball, Klopp's teams move it forward quickly, often with a few direct passes toward the forwards. The wide forwards run into space and the full-backs push on to support. The aim is to reach the penalty area while the opponent is still recovering. This style is close to counter-attacking, but it starts from a press high up the pitch.

## What are the risks and criticisms?
A high press needs a lot of running, so it is hard to keep up for a whole season. If the opponent passes through the press, there is open space behind the midfield and defence. Teams that sit deep in a low block can also make it hard to find the space the style needs.
