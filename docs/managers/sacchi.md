# Arrigo Sacchi: Pressing and the Compact Line at Milan

## Summary
Arrigo Sacchi is an Italian football manager. He is best known for his time at AC Milan in the late 1980s and early 1990s, and he later managed the Italy national team. His Milan team is famous for pressing, zonal marking and a compact team shape, at a time when man-marking was still common in Italian football.

## What is his philosophy?
Sacchi built his team around moving as a unit. The defenders, midfielders and forwards stayed close together, so there was little space between the lines for the opponent to use. The whole team pressed the ball and moved up or down the pitch together. He also asked his players to defend in zones, instead of following one opponent. Collective play and team organisation mattered more to him than individual roles.

## How did his Milan team play?
Sacchi managed Milan from 1987 to 1991. The team played a 4-4-2 with a high defensive line and used the offside trap. When the opponent had the ball, the players pressed to win it back in the opponent's half. The defence included Franco Baresi, Paolo Maldini, Alessandro Costacurta and Mauro Tassotti. Frank Rijkaard, Ruud Gullit and Marco van Basten were key players in midfield and attack.

## What did Milan win under Sacchi?
Milan won Serie A in 1987-88. The team won the European Cup in 1988-89 and again in 1989-90. Fabio Capello took over as Milan manager in 1991.

## What did he do with Italy?
Sacchi managed the Italy national team from 1991 to 1996. Italy reached the final of the 1994 World Cup, where it lost to Brazil on penalties.

## Why is he influential?
Many coaches have copied the idea of a compact team that presses together and defends in zones. His Milan side showed that a team could defend high up the pitch and still be very organised at the back. His ideas are linked to modern pressing and to the high line.

## What are the main criticisms?
His style demands a lot of fitness and concentration from every player. A high line leaves space behind the defence if the press fails or an offside trap is broken. It also relies on all players moving at the same time, so one mistake can open a gap.
