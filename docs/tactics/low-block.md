# Low Block Defending

## Summary
A low block is a way of defending in which the team drops deep into its own half and stays compact near its own goal. Instead of pressing high, it gives the opponent the ball in less dangerous areas and tries to protect the space in front of goal. It is a defensive choice, not a formation, and teams can use it for a whole match or only for parts of it.

## What is a low block?
In a low block, the defensive line sits close to the team's own penalty area, and the midfielders and forwards drop back behind the ball. The team has many players between the ball and the goal. The opponent can keep possession, but has little room to play through the middle or behind the defence.

## How does the team set up?
Common shapes are 4-4-2 and 4-5-1, with two tight lines of four or a line of four and a line of five. The distances between the lines are short, so there are no gaps for a pass to enter. The midfield line protects the space just in front of the defence, and a No. 6 often stays in that area. The team shifts sideways together as the ball moves.

## What is the aim?
The first aim is to deny space in the central areas and near the penalty area, so the opponent has to play wide or shoot from distance. The second aim is to win the ball and attack the space the opponent has left behind. Many teams use a low block when they expect to have less of the ball or want to protect a lead.

## What does a team need to defend deep well?
Discipline matters most. Players must keep their positions and not chase the ball out of the block. The team needs strong defenders in the air to deal with crosses, and good communication to pass on opponents who move between zones. Concentration is important, because the team may defend for long periods.

## How does a low block attack?
A team in a low block often attacks with quick counter-attacks. When it wins the ball, a long pass or a fast run forward can use the space left by the opponent's attackers. A forward who can hold the ball up helps the rest of the team move up the pitch.

## What are the risks?
The opponent has the ball for long periods, so pressure builds up over time. Crosses, shots from distance and set pieces become the main threats. It is hard to clear the ball and relieve pressure, and one mistake close to goal can be costly. Players can also tire from defending for so long.

## Which teams are known for it?
Diego Simeone's Atlético Madrid is often cited for compact, disciplined defending in a 4-4-2. José Mourinho's Inter is also known for deep defending in its 2010 Champions League semi-final against Barcelona.
