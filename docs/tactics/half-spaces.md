# Half-Spaces and Overloads

## Summary
A half-space is a vertical lane of the pitch between the wide area and the centre. An overload is a situation in which a team has more players than the opponent in one area. Teams use half-spaces and overloads to create room for passes and shots against an organised defence. They are ideas about positioning, not formations, and teams can use them in a 4-3-3 or in other shapes.

## What is a half-space?
Many coaches divide the pitch into five vertical lanes. The two outer lanes are the wide areas, the middle lane is the centre, and the two lanes in between are the half-spaces. A player in a half-space is not on the touchline and not in the middle of the pitch. From there, the player can pass inside, pass wide, run behind the defence or shoot.

## Why are half-spaces useful?
A player in a half-space is hard to mark. A centre-back may not want to leave the middle, and a full-back may not want to leave the wide area. This leaves the player with time to receive the ball and turn. The angle also gives more options than the touchline, where the pitch limits the player's choices.

## What is an overload?
An overload means having more players than the opponent in a zone, for example four attackers against three defenders. The extra player gives the team a free passing option. The team can keep the ball more easily in that area, and the defenders must decide who to follow. A team can create an overload by moving players into the zone, such as a midfielder or a false 9 who drops or drifts to one side.

## How do overloads and half-spaces work together?
A team can fill a half-space and the wide area on one side, so the opponent must move across to defend. Once the defenders have shifted, a quick switch of play to the other side finds a teammate with more room. A No. 6 behind the ball helps the team keep its shape and pass between the two sides.

## What does a team need to use them well?
Players need good positioning, so they stay in the right lane and do not crowd each other. They also need a good first touch and accurate passing in tight spaces. The team must keep enough players in other areas, so the pitch stays wide and the opponent cannot defend narrow.

## What are the risks?
Too many players on one side leave the other side empty, and a lost ball can lead to a counter-attack into that space. If players stand in the same lane, they block each other and the overload does not help.

## Which teams are known for it?
Pep Guardiola's teams are closely linked with using half-spaces and overloads as part of positional play.
