# Transitions and Rest Defence

## Summary
A transition is the moment when possession changes from one team to the other. When a team wins the ball, it moves from defending to attacking. When it loses the ball, it moves from attacking to defending. Rest defence is the group of players a team keeps back while it attacks, so that it is protected if it loses the ball. Both are about how a team behaves in the seconds around a change of possession.

## What is a transition?
A transition happens whenever the ball changes sides. In the first seconds, both teams are out of their normal shape. The team that has just won the ball has a chance to attack space before the opponent recovers. The team that has just lost it must decide quickly whether to press the ball or fall back. How well a team handles these moments can decide a match.

## What is rest defence?
Rest defence describes the players who stay in a safe position behind the ball while the rest of the team attacks. They are not defending at that moment, but they are ready if the ball is lost. Their job is to stop a counter-attack before it starts. Rest defence is planned before the ball is lost, not after it.

## How does a team set up its rest defence?
Teams usually keep the centre-backs back, and often a No. 6 close to them. Together they cover the central space and are ready to meet a long pass or a fast attacker. Some teams also keep one full-back deeper while the other goes forward. The players stay in positions where they can see the ball and the opponent's forwards, so they can react quickly.

## How does it connect to counter-pressing?
The players in front of the ball can counter-press right after a loss, and the rest defence stays behind as cover. If the press wins the ball, the team can attack again. If the press is passed, the players at the back already have a safe shape. The two parts work together, so the team can press without leaving the whole pitch open.

## What are the risks?
If too many players go forward, the rest defence is thin, and a lost ball can lead to a counter-attack against only a few defenders. If too many stay back, the attack lacks support. The team must also keep the distances between players short, so the cover is close enough to help.

## Which teams are known for it?
Guardiola's teams are often linked with careful rest defence, with centre-backs and a No. 6 staying behind the ball while the team attacks. Klopp's Liverpool is known for a quick reaction when the ball is lost.
