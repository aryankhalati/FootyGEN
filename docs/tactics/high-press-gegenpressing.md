# High Press and Counter-Pressing

## Summary
A high press is a way of defending in which the team puts pressure on the opponent close to the opponent's goal, instead of waiting in its own half. Counter-pressing, also called gegenpressing, is immediate pressure to win the ball back right after the team loses it. Both are forms of pressing, but they start from different situations. A high press starts when the opponent has the ball in their own half. Counter-pressing starts the moment possession is lost.

## What is a high press?
In a high press, the forwards close down the goalkeeper and centre-backs and block the passing lanes. The midfielders stay close behind to cut central passes and move up when the ball goes into a pressing trap. The aim is to win the ball high up the pitch, or to force a poor pass. A high press is a choice, not part of a formation. The same team can also defend in a mid-block or a low block.

## What are pressing triggers?
A pressing trigger is a signal that tells the team to press at the same moment. Common triggers are a backward pass, a slow pass or a poor first touch. When the trigger happens, the players move together to close space and cut off options. If the team has no clear trigger, players press at different times and the opponent can play around them.

## What is counter-pressing?
Counter-pressing means pressing immediately after losing the ball, instead of retreating into a defensive shape. The goal is to win the ball back before the opponent can organise a counter-attack. It also stops the opponent from using the space left by the team's attackers. Counter-pressing needs nearby cover, so players must stay close enough to the ball to apply pressure at once. If the first wave is bypassed, the team must recover quickly.

## How are the two different?
A high press is planned around where the opponent builds up. Counter-pressing happens in the moment, after the team has lost the ball while attacking. Teams often use both.

## What does a team need to press well?
Pressing works only if the whole team moves together. The distances between the lines must stay short, so there is little space between defence, midfield and attack. A player who presses alone lets the opponent play around them. Fitness matters too, because pressing requires repeated sprints and quick recovery. The team must also agree who presses and who covers.

## What are the risks?
If the opponent beats the press with a long pass or a quick combination, the space behind the pressing players is open. A team with a high defensive line is especially exposed to a pass in behind. Tired players cannot keep up the same intensity, so the press can become weaker later in a match. The team must also be ready to drop into a block if the press fails.

## Which teams are known for it?
Jürgen Klopp's Liverpool used the 4-3-3 for intense pressing, counter-pressing and fast vertical attacks after winning the ball. Guardiola's Barcelona also used immediate pressure after losing the ball, as part of its patient possession game. Klopp is closely associated with gegenpressing.
