# Man-Marking and Zonal Marking

## Summary
Marking is the way defenders deal with opponents who do not have the ball. In man-marking, each defender follows one opponent. In zonal marking, each defender protects an area of the pitch and deals with any opponent who enters it. Both are defensive choices, not formations, and teams can use them in open play and on set pieces. Many teams use a mix of the two.

## What is man-marking?
In man-marking, a defender is given one opponent and stays close to that player wherever they go. The aim is to stop that player from receiving the ball or turning with it. The defender's position depends on the opponent, not on the shape of the team. This system is clear and easy to explain, because every defender knows who they are responsible for.

## What is zonal marking?
In zonal marking, the team defends in lines, and each player has an area to protect. When an opponent moves into the area, the defender marks them. When the opponent leaves, the defender passes them on to a teammate and watches the next player. The defenders move together as a unit, which helps keep the team compact and the distances between players short.

## What are the strengths of each system?
Man-marking can shut down a dangerous player and leaves less doubt about who is responsible. Zonal marking keeps the team's shape, so defenders are not pulled out of position by the opponent's movement. It also helps defenders face the ball and attack it, instead of following a runner and losing sight of play.

## What are the weaknesses?
Man-marking can be dragged out of shape. If an attacker drops deep or drifts wide, the defender must decide whether to follow, and gaps can open behind. Zonal marking depends on communication. If two defenders both think a player belongs to the other, an opponent can be left free. Attackers can also run into a zone at speed, which gives the defender less time to react.

## How do teams mix the two?
Many teams combine the systems. In open play, a team may defend in zones but pick up a player closely when that player enters a key area. On set pieces, some defenders may mark an opponent while others guard a zone. A mixed system tries to keep the strengths of both, but every player must know which job they have.

## Which teams are known for it?
Italian teams of the 1960s were known for man-marking, often with a spare defender behind the line, called a sweeper. Arrigo Sacchi's AC Milan is the best-known example of a team built around zonal marking and a compact line.
