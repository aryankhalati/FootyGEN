# High Defensive Line and the Offside Trap

## Summary
A high defensive line means the back players defend far from their own goal, close to the halfway line. The offside trap is a tactic in which the defenders move forward together at the right moment, so an attacker is left in an offside position when the ball is played. Both are defensive choices, not formations. Teams can use them in a 4-4-2, a 4-3-3 or other shapes.

## What is a high defensive line?
In a high line, the defenders stay far up the pitch, and the midfielders and forwards stay close in front of them. This makes the space between the lines small and gives the opponent little room to play in. It also helps the team press, because the whole team is close to the ball. The space behind the defenders is large, so the line must be well organised.

## What is the offside trap?
A player is in an offside position if they are nearer to the opponent's goal line than both the ball and the second-last opponent when the ball is played to them. It is only an offence if the player then becomes involved in play. In an offside trap, the defenders step forward together just before the pass is played, so the attacker is left in an offside position. The referee team then gives a free kick to the defending team.

## How does the line work together?
The defenders must move as one line, so one player who stays back keeps the attacker onside. The line also needs a clear signal for when to step up, often a call from one defender or the goalkeeper. The defenders watch the player with the ball. If that player is under pressure and cannot play a good pass, the line can push up with more confidence.

## What does a team need to defend this way?
Defenders need good concentration and communication, and enough pace to recover if a ball goes behind them. The goalkeeper often needs to leave the area to clear long passes, which is why a sweeper-keeper suits this style. Pressure on the ball matters as well, because a passer with time can pick out a runner.

## What are the risks?
A well-timed ball over the top or a through ball can leave one attacker alone against the goalkeeper. One defender who is out of line, or a mistake by the officials, can turn a trap into a goal. Tired defenders also find it harder to recover quickly.

## Which teams are known for it?
Arrigo Sacchi's AC Milan is the best-known example of a high line and offside trap in a 4-4-2. Guardiola's teams also defend with a high line while pressing.
