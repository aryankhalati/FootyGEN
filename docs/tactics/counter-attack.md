# Counter-Attacking

## Summary
Counter-attacking is a way of attacking in which the team wins the ball and moves it forward quickly, before the opponent can get back into a defensive shape. It uses the space the opponent leaves when they push players forward. It is a style of play, not a formation, and teams can use it for a whole match or only in certain moments.

## What is a counter-attack?
A counter-attack starts when a team wins the ball and attacks at once, with few passes and a lot of speed. The opponent has players high up the pitch, so there is open space behind them. The team tries to reach that space and shoot before the opponent has recovered. The moment of winning the ball is called a transition.

## How does a counter-attack work?
The first pass after winning the ball is important. It often goes forward to a teammate who can run with the ball or play it on to a runner. Players in attack make runs into the space behind the defence, and others support from midfield. The team usually plays with fewer passes than in a possession-based move, and often attacks with only a few players.

## What does a team need to counter well?
Speed is a clear advantage, especially in the forwards and wide players. The team also needs players who can pass accurately under pressure and make a good decision in the first seconds. A forward who can hold the ball up gives teammates time to join the attack. Players must also be ready to move forward as soon as the ball is won.

## When do teams use it?
Teams often counter-attack when they defend deep, such as in a low block, because they win the ball in their own half and the opponent has players forward. It also suits a team that expects to have less of the ball. Counter-attacking is not only for weaker teams, and teams that mostly keep possession can use it too.

## How is it different from counter-pressing?
Counter-pressing is about winning the ball back quickly after losing it. A counter-attack starts after the ball is won, and is about moving it forward quickly. A team can win the ball with a counter-press and then counter-attack straight away.

## What are the risks?
If the first pass is poor, the team loses the ball again with its players spread out. The opponent can then attack the space left behind. A team that relies on counters can struggle when the opponent defends deep and leaves no space to run into.

## Which teams are known for it?
Mourinho's Real Madrid used counter-attacking football when they won La Liga in 2011-12, with a record 100 points.
