# Possession Football and Tiki-Taka

## Summary
Possession football is a style of play in which the team tries to keep the ball for long periods and control the match through passing. Tiki-taka is a well-known version of it, built on short, quick passes and constant movement. It is a style, not a formation, and teams can use it in a 4-3-3 or in other shapes.

## What is possession football?
In possession football, the team treats the ball as its main way of defending and attacking. If the team has the ball, the opponent cannot score. The team passes patiently, moves the opponent around and waits for a gap to appear. The aim is to control the match, not only to keep the ball for its own sake.

## What is tiki-taka?
Tiki-taka is a style based on short passes, often played first time, and players moving into new positions after each pass. The ball moves quickly between players who stay close to each other, so there are always passing options. The name is linked mainly with Barcelona under Pep Guardiola and with the Spain national team of the same period.

## How does the team move the ball?
Players stay close together so each one has several nearby teammates to pass to. These small groups are often called triangles. A No. 6 usually sits in front of the defence to receive the ball and start moves. The team also uses the width of the pitch, to pull the opponent out of shape and open space in the middle. A false 9 can drop into midfield, which gives the team an extra player in the centre.

## What does a team need to play this way?
Players must be comfortable on the ball, even under pressure. Good first touch, accurate passing and awareness of space are all important. The goalkeeper and defenders also need to pass well, because the team starts moves from the back. Players must keep moving, so that the passer always has an option.

## How does the team win the ball back?
Teams that play this way often press straight after losing the ball. This is called counter-pressing. It keeps the opponent away from the goal and lets the team start passing again quickly.

## What are the risks?
Passing without a clear aim can be slow and easy to defend against. Opponents who defend deep in a low block leave little space to pass through. A lost ball in the team's own half can lead to a dangerous counter-attack, because the players are positioned forward.

## Which teams are known for it?
Guardiola's Barcelona is the best-known example, with Xavi and Iniesta in midfield and Busquets as the No. 6. Spain won Euro 2008, the 2010 World Cup and Euro 2012 with a similar passing style.
