# 3-5-2 Formation

## Summary
The 3-5-2 is a formation with three centre-backs, five midfielders and two strikers. The five midfielders are two wing-backs and three central midfielders. The numbers describe the starting shape, not where players stand in every phase. In possession the wing-backs push high and it can become a 3-3-4 or 3-2-5 type structure, and without the ball it drops into a 5-3-2. It trades width in defence for numbers in the centre, and it asks a lot of its wing-backs.

## Shape and roles
In the 3-5-2, the three centre-backs split the defensive line into a central defender and two wider ones. The wing-backs cover the whole flank, defending as full-backs and attacking as wingers. The three central midfielders usually include one holding player in the middle and two others who support defence and attack. The two strikers work as a partnership. The 3-5-2 does not require the three centre-backs or the three midfielders to have identical jobs.

## Back three variations
The back three in a 3-5-2 can be set up in different ways. In a ball-playing version, the middle centre-back or the wider ones step forward with the ball into midfield. In a more defensive version, a libero sweeps behind two markers and rarely leaves the line. In a flat version, all three defend the box side by side and the wing-backs do the wide work. The wider centre-backs often cover the channels beside the box, which protects the space behind the wing-backs. The right mix depends on how mobile the three defenders are.

## In possession: build-up
The 3-5-2 builds from the goalkeeper and the back three, which gives three passing options across the first line. Against two pressing strikers, the team has an extra player in build-up, so one centre-back is usually free. The middle midfielder drops into the gap to receive, or one of the wider centre-backs carries the ball forward. The wing-backs hold their width high to pin the opposition's wide players. The aim of build-up is to draw the press in and play through or around it into midfield.

## In possession: wing-backs
In the 3-5-2, the wing-backs provide almost all the width. Because the back three covers the centre, they can push high, almost level with the strikers. They deliver crosses, overlap, and take on defenders one-versus-one. If one wing-back attacks, the other often stays slightly deeper for balance, though some teams push both. The wing-backs have a very heavy workload, since they must cover the whole flank for 90 minutes, so they need to be fit and strong in both directions.

## In possession: central midfield and strikers
The three central midfielders give the 3-5-2 a numerical advantage in the centre against a two-man midfield and an equal fight against a three. One of them can step forward to link with the strikers or arrive late at the edge of the box. The two strikers can pair a target man with a runner, or a poacher with a creator. They pin the opposition's centre-backs and attack the box when the wing-backs cross. A midfielder who drops to help build-up leaves the others to cover.

## In possession: final third
The 3-5-2 creates chances through wing-back crosses, passes into the strikers, runs from the central midfielders and combinations between a wing-back and a striker who drifts wide. With two strikers and a wing-back or two arriving, the team can have four or five players in the box. Against a deep block, wide play and crosses are the main routes. Against a high line, a pass in behind for a striker or a wing-back running beyond is often more valuable than slow passing in front of it.

## Rest defence
The 3-5-2 has natural rest defence, because the three centre-backs are already in place behind the ball. If the wing-backs go high and possession is lost, the back three and at least one central midfielder can still defend the central area. The danger is the space on the flanks. A counter-attack down the side catches the team when the wing-back is far upfield and the wider centre-back has to cover a big gap. Teams usually keep one wing-back deeper to reduce that risk.

## Out of possession: pressing
The 3-5-2 presses with its two strikers on the opposition centre-backs, while the central midfielders mark the middle. The wing-backs step up to press the opposition's full-backs, so the shape can become a 3-4-3 in the press. The back three can step out aggressively to win the ball in midfield. Pressing works only if the whole team moves together, usually on triggers such as a backward pass or a poor touch. If the wing-backs press too early, big gaps open behind them.

## Out of possession: defensive blocks
The 3-5-2 defends in a mid-block or low block, usually as a 5-3-2. The wing-backs drop back into the back line, which gives five defenders across the pitch, and the three central midfielders sit just ahead. The two strikers stay high to block passes or drop to help the midfield. The block is compact in the centre and hard to break down through the middle. Opposition teams usually try to attack the wide areas, where the wing-backs and wider centre-backs defend.

## Transitions
When a 3-5-2 wins the ball, the strikers make forward runs and the wing-backs sprint down the flanks, which gives the team several outlets in quick attacks. A central midfielder carries or passes the ball forward. When the team loses the ball, it can counter-press with the strikers and midfielders, or drop into the back five. The strong central presence helps recovery, but the wide spaces are the danger area during transitions.

## Strengths
The 3-5-2 has a solid base, with three centre-backs that become five defenders without the ball. It outnumbers the opposition in central midfield, which helps control the middle. Two strikers give the team a constant outlet and two forwards to attack crosses. The wing-backs provide width and attacking threat from deep. It is also well suited to teams with strong centre-backs and few good wingers.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness of the 3-5-2 is the space behind and beside the wing-backs, which opponents can attack when they are high up the pitch. The wing-backs have a huge workload and tire late in games. The shape needs three good centre-backs, and a weak back three is badly exposed. Against a team with two strong wingers plus attacking full-backs, the wing-backs can be stretched. Two strikers who are too similar can also leave no link between midfield and attack.

## Related shapes
The 3-5-2 comes in several versions. The 3-4-1-2 has four midfielders, with the fifth player playing as a No. 10 behind two strikers. The 3-4-3 uses the same back three with three forwards instead of a third central midfielder. The 5-3-2 is usually the defensive version of the same shape, with the wing-backs dropping into the back line. The 3-1-4-2 uses one deep midfielder in front of the back three. A label can refer to the starting shape or the defensive block, and these can differ within one match.

## Example: Beckenbauer's West Germany
Franz Beckenbauer's West Germany won the 1990 World Cup using a 3-5-2. Klaus Augenthaler was the sweeper, with Guido Buchwald and Jürgen Kohler beside him. Andreas Brehme played left wing-back, and Thomas Berthold started the final at right wing-back before Stefan Reuter replaced him on 73 minutes. Lothar Matthäus, Pierre Littbarski and Thomas Häßler formed the midfield, with Jürgen Klinsmann and Rudi Völler up front. West Germany combined a solid back line, a strong midfield and direct play to the strikers.

## Example: Conte's Juventus
Antonio Conte's Juventus won three straight Serie A titles from 2011-12 to 2013-14, and the 3-5-2 became their identity. Conte started his first season with a back four, then played Barzagli, Bonucci and Chiellini together as a back three for the first time in a 3-3 draw at Napoli on 29 November 2011. From 2012-13 it was the default shape. Andrea Pirlo was the deep playmaker, with Arturo Vidal and Claudio Marchisio beside him. Stephan Lichtsteiner and Kwadwo Asamoah were the regular wing-backs from 2012-13, and Carlos Tévez and Fernando Llorente led the attack in 2013-14. Juventus built from the back three and attacked through quick wing-back play.
