# 4-1-4-1 Formation

## Summary
The 4-1-4-1 is a formation with four defenders, one holding midfielder, four midfielders and one striker. The defence is two centre-backs and two full-backs, a single No. 6 sits in front of them, a line of four midfielders sits ahead of him, and a lone striker leads the attack. The numbers describe the starting shape, not where players stand in every phase. In possession the wide midfielders push high and it often becomes a 4-3-3 or 2-3-5 type structure, and without the ball it defends as a compact 4-1-4-1 or 4-5-1. It is known for protecting the centre and for being flexible.

## Shape and roles
In the 4-1-4-1, the holding midfielder (the No. 6) sits alone in front of the back four. He screens the centre-backs, wins the ball and starts attacks. The two central midfielders in the four support him, one usually more defensive and the other more attacking. The two wide midfielders provide width, cross, cut inside and track back. The lone striker holds the ball up, runs in behind or presses the centre-backs. The full-backs defend the flanks and overlap when the wide midfielders move inside.

## The No. 6 in the 4-1-4-1
The 4-1-4-1 depends on its single holding midfielder, who has to do the work of a double pivot alone. A destroyer wins duels and protects the back four. A deep-lying playmaker controls tempo and switches play with his passing. A ball-winning midfielder who can also carry the ball does both jobs. A No. 6 who is strong in the air and quick across the ground can cover the space left by attacking full-backs. If the No. 6 is weak, the whole shape is exposed, because nobody else sits in front of the centre-backs.

## In possession: build-up
The 4-1-4-1 builds from the goalkeeper and centre-backs, with the No. 6 showing in front of them for the pass. He can also drop between the centre-backs, which lets the full-backs push higher. The two central midfielders move into the gaps between the opposition lines to receive. Against a high press, the team can go long to the lone striker or to the wide midfielders. The aim of build-up is to draw the press forward and then find a free player in midfield.

## In possession: wide midfielders and full-backs
In the 4-1-4-1, the wide midfielders either stay wide or cut inside. A wide midfielder who stays wide stretches the opposition and creates one-versus-one situations, while the full-back stays deeper or underlaps. A wide midfielder who cuts inside joins the central midfielders, and the full-back overlaps to supply the width. When both wide players push high, the shape becomes a 4-3-3 with the central midfielders and No. 6 behind them. The full-backs are essential to the width, so teams choose which one attacks and which one stays back.

## In possession: the lone striker
The lone striker in a 4-1-4-1 plays a difficult role. A target man holds the ball up and brings the midfield forward to support him. A mobile striker runs in behind and stretches the defence. A false nine drops into midfield and lets the wide players and central midfielders run beyond him. The striker needs fast support, because with four midfielders behind him there are many players who can arrive, but only if they move forward quickly. If support is slow, he becomes isolated against two centre-backs.

## In possession: final third
The 4-1-4-1 creates chances through runs from the central midfielders, wide midfielders cutting inside, overlapping full-backs, passes to the striker's feet and crosses. With four midfielders behind the striker, the team can arrive in numbers at the edge of the box. Against a compact defence, quick passes between the central midfielders and a wide player can open space. Against a high line, a ball in behind for the striker or a wide runner is often more effective than slow passing. The No. 6 stays back and does not usually join the box.

## Rest defence
The 4-1-4-1 has natural rest defence because the No. 6 already sits in front of the centre-backs. If the full-backs and wide midfielders go forward, the two centre-backs and the No. 6 still form a central trio against a counter-attack. The weakness is that the No. 6 is alone. If a central midfielder pushes up and the No. 6 is bypassed, the centre-backs are exposed, so teams usually keep one central midfielder close to him when the other advances.

## Out of possession: pressing
The 4-1-4-1 presses with the lone striker on the centre-backs or the goalkeeper, with the wide midfielders closing the full-backs and the central midfielders stepping up behind. In a high press, the formation often turns into a 4-3-3, with the wide midfielders joining the striker in the front line. The No. 6 holds the middle and covers the passes into the opposition's midfield. Pressing works only if the whole team moves together, usually on triggers such as a backward pass or a poor touch. A striker who presses alone does not stop the opponent playing around him.

## Out of possession: defensive blocks
The 4-1-4-1 is known for its compact mid-block and low block. The team defends in a bank of four defenders, with the No. 6 in front of them, a bank of four midfielders ahead of him and the striker on top. This closes the space between the lines, so the opponent's No. 10 has little room to receive the ball. The wide midfielders tuck in to protect the middle and drop back to help the full-backs. When the No. 6 steps up into the midfield line, the shape becomes a 4-5-1. The lone striker blocks the pass to the opposing holding midfielder or stays high as a counter-attacking outlet.

## Transitions
When a 4-1-4-1 wins the ball, the central midfielders and wide players run forward in support of the striker, and the No. 6 provides a safe pass if there is no forward option. The wide midfielders can sprint into open channels. When the team loses the ball, it can counter-press with the nearby midfielders, or drop straight back into its two compact lines. The No. 6 is the key to defensive transition, because he can cut off the first counter-attacking pass before the back four is stretched.

## Strengths
The 4-1-4-1 protects the back four with a dedicated holding midfielder and a compact midfield line. It has a strong central presence, with five players in the middle of the pitch. It is flexible, because it can turn into a 4-3-3 in attack and a 4-5-1 in defence without changing the starting players. Its midfield four offers width and central support, which suits a team that wants control without committing too many players forward.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness of the 4-1-4-1 is the lone striker, who can be isolated by two centre-backs if the midfield support is slow. The single No. 6 can be overloaded by a team with several central runners or a No. 10 who moves into his zone. The team can sit too deep and give up territory if the wide midfielders do not support the attack. The wide midfielders must work hard both ways, and the full-backs are exposed if they do not track back. The team may also lack a clear creative link in the final third.

## Related shapes
The 4-1-4-1 is closely related to other formations. The 4-3-3 is similar, but has three forwards and a three-man midfield in attack, and the two shapes often swap within a match. The 4-5-1 is its defensive version, with the No. 6 stepping up into the midfield line. The 4-2-3-1 has a double pivot and a No. 10 where the 4-1-4-1 has a single No. 6 and a flat four. A label can refer to the starting formation, the attacking structure or the defensive block, and these can differ within one match.

## Example: Aragonés' Spain at Euro 2008
Luis Aragonés' Spain won Euro 2008, and in the final against Germany they lined up in a 4-1-4-1 (some reports write it as a 4-5-1). Marcos Senna was the holding midfielder, with Iniesta, Xavi, Cesc Fàbregas and David Silva in the midfield four. Fernando Torres was the lone striker and scored the only goal in the 33rd minute. Fàbregas started in place of the injured David Villa, and earlier in the tournament, including the semi-final against Russia, Spain had played a 4-4-2 with two strikers. Spain kept the ball with short passing, and the midfield four moved around Senna. The team showed that a 4-1-4-1 can be built on possession and technical midfielders, not only on defending.
