# 4-3-3 Formation

## Summary
The 4-3-3 is a formation with four defenders, three midfielders and three forwards. The defence is two centre-backs and two full-backs, the midfield is usually one deeper player and two more advanced ones, and the attack is a left winger, a central striker and a right winger. The numbers describe the starting shape, not where players stand in every phase. In possession it can become a 2-3-5 or 3-2-5, and without the ball it often drops into a 4-1-4-1 or 4-5-1. It is often linked with possession football, but teams also use it to press high, counter-attack or defend in a block.

## Shape and roles
In the 4-3-3, the holding midfielder (the No. 6) sits in front of the back four. He gives a passing outlet, protects the centre and links defence to midfield. The two central midfielders (the No. 8s) connect the lines, carry the ball forward and make runs into the box. The wingers provide width, take on defenders and attack space behind the defence. The central striker occupies centre-backs, or drops to link play. The 4-3-3 does not require three midfielders with identical jobs.

## Midfield variations
The 4-3-3 midfield comes in a few versions. The most common is one No. 6 with two No. 8s, which gives a clear balance of defence, progression and attack. A second version uses two deeper midfielders and one advanced one, which can look like a 4-2-3-1 during play. A third is asymmetric: one No. 8 pushes into the box while the other stays deeper, or one creates while the other presses and covers. In many 4-3-3 sides the No. 8s take turns advancing so the centre is never left empty.

## In possession: build-up
The 4-3-3 builds from the goalkeeper and centre-backs. The No. 6 shows in front of them, moves to one side to open a passing lane, or drops between the centre-backs. If the No. 6 drops, a No. 8 often moves deeper or stays high to keep a presence in the middle. The aim of build-up is to draw the opposition press forward and then find a free player beyond it. If the central routes are blocked, the team can bypass pressure with a longer pass to a winger, striker or advanced midfielder.

## In possession: wingers and full-backs
In the 4-3-3, wingers stretch the opposition and create one-versus-one situations near the touchline. That width opens central space for the midfielders and striker. Full-backs give several options: an overlapping full-back runs outside the winger, an underlapping full-back moves into the inside channel, and an inverted full-back steps into central midfield. If the winger cuts inside, the full-back usually supplies the width. If the full-back moves inside, the winger stays wide. A team can use a different solution on each flank.

## In possession: the central striker
The 4-3-3 striker can play in two main ways. A traditional No. 9 occupies the centre-backs, runs in behind and gives the team a target for crosses. A false nine starts as the central forward but repeatedly drops into midfield. This creates a dilemma for the defence: if a centre-back follows, space opens behind him, and if he stays, the false nine receives between the lines and gives the team an extra midfielder. A striker who only occasionally drops deep is not necessarily a false nine. The wide forwards and midfield runners must attack the space the false nine leaves.

## In possession: final third
The 4-3-3 creates chances through winger and full-back combinations, passes into the striker, late runs from the No. 8s, switches of play, crosses, cut-backs and dribbles. Against a crowded centre, keeping a winger wide and switching the ball quickly can create a one-versus-one. Against wide full-backs, an inside forward or midfielder can find space in the half-space, the channel between the centre and the touchline. Against a high defensive line, runs in behind are worth more than short combinations in front of it.

## Rest defence
The 4-3-3 needs a plan for losing the ball while attacking. Rest defence means the players kept behind the ball to stop a counter-attack, usually the two centre-backs plus one or more midfielders or a full-back. If both full-backs go high and the No. 6 is left alone, the team is exposed to counters through the wide channels. Which players stay back depends on which full-back is attacking and which No. 8 has advanced.

## Out of possession: pressing
The 4-3-3 presses from the front, with the three forwards closing the goalkeeper and centre-backs and blocking passing lanes. The midfield three stay close behind to cut central passes and move up when the ball goes into a pressing trap. Pressing works only if the whole team moves together, usually on triggers such as a backward pass, a slow pass or a poor first touch. A winger who presses alone lets the opponent play around him.

## Out of possession: defensive blocks
Pressing high is a choice, not part of the 4-3-3 shape. The same formation can defend in a mid-block or low block, with the front three screening forward passes and the midfield protecting the centre. When the wingers drop back alongside the midfield, the 4-3-3 becomes a 4-5-1 that protects the flanks. With the No. 6 in front of the back four and four midfielders ahead, it becomes a 4-1-4-1. The midfield three must keep the right distance from the defence, or opponents receive freely between the lines.

## Transitions
When a 4-3-3 wins the ball, the wingers sprint into open channels, the striker offers a forward target and a No. 8 carries or passes forward. If there is no safe forward option, the team keeps the ball and rebuilds. When it loses the ball, it can counter-press, meaning immediate pressure to win it back, or retreat into shape. Counter-pressing stops the opponent exploiting the space left by attackers, but it needs nearby cover. If the first wave is bypassed, the team must recover quickly.

## Strengths
The 4-3-3 has natural width through two wingers, who stretch the defence and create one-versus-one chances. Its three midfielders form passing triangles and offer several routes through the centre. The front three can press the first phase of build-up. The forward line is flexible, because wingers can stay wide or move inside and the striker can run in behind or drop. The system adapts through full-back positioning and midfield roles without changing the nominal formation.

## Weaknesses
The main weakness of the 4-3-3 is the space behind advanced full-backs if possession is lost. A lone No. 6 can be overloaded when both No. 8s push too high or opponents put several players around him. Gaps can open between the lines if the forwards press and the midfield cannot follow. Roles like the false nine or inverted full-back only work if teammates adjust their positions to keep width and balance. If the wingers are isolated, the team can rely too much on crosses and individual dribbling.

## Related shapes
The 4-3-3 is closely related to other formations. The 4-1-4-1 and 4-5-1 are usually its defensive versions, with the same players in different positions. The 4-2-3-1 is similar, but has two deeper midfielders and a clearly defined attacking midfielder behind the striker. A 4-3-3 with two deeper midfielders can resemble it during play. A label can refer to the starting formation, the attacking structure or the defensive block, and these can differ within one match.

## Example: Guardiola's Barcelona
The 4-3-3 is best known from Pep Guardiola's Barcelona (2008 to 2012). Busquets played as the No. 6, with Xavi and Iniesta in midfield. Messi increasingly played as a false nine from 2009, dropping into midfield and letting the wide forwards and midfield runners attack the space. Barcelona used the 4-3-3 for patient possession, short passing, positional rotations and immediate pressure after losing the ball. The aim was to move the opponent's shape and find free players, not to pass for its own sake.

## Example: Klopp's Liverpool
Jürgen Klopp's Liverpool used the 4-3-3 differently in 2018-19. Salah and Mané were wide forwards who attacked the space behind defences, and Firmino was a mobile centre-forward who dropped, pressed and linked play. Fabinho, Henderson and Wijnaldum formed the usual midfield, though selection varied. Alexander-Arnold and Robertson gave width and crossing from full-back, so the midfield and centre-backs had to cover for them. Liverpool used the 4-3-3 for intense pressing, counter-pressing and fast vertical attacks after winning the ball.
